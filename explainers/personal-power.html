A session-by-session guide to Tony Robbins's 30-day program

Personal Power,
Explained

Tony Robbins's Personal Power is a 30-day audio program, first released in 1989 and sold for years on late-night TV, that turned him into a household name. It isn't a book you read once; it's 20 sessions spread across 30 days, each with an assignment to do that day. This guide takes them one at a time: what the session teaches, what it asks you to do, and a lab where you try the idea yourself.

Days 1 to 4 · sessions 1 to 4

The Science of Change

Robbins opens the program with his core claim: change can happen in a moment, once you understand what drives your behaviour. The first four days lay out the formula for getting results, the two forces behind every decision, and the step-by-step method he calls Neuro-Associative Conditioning.

The idea behind this stretch

You don't lack ability. You have habits wired to feelings, and anything wired can be rewired.

Session 1 · Day 1 · The formula behind every result

The Key to Personal Power

In short

Personal power is the ability to take action. Robbins boils success down to a four-step loop: know what you want, act, notice what happens, and keep changing your approach until you get it.

The big ideas

  • Knowledge is only potential power. Most people already know what they should do. Power comes from acting on it, which is why the whole program is built around daily assignments rather than information.
  • The Ultimate Success Formula. Decide exactly what you want, take massive action, develop the sensory acuity to notice whether it's working, and have the flexibility to change course. Then repeat.
  • Flexibility beats being right. The person with the most options usually wins. If one approach isn't working, a dozen more are available; the only real failure is to keep doing the same thing and expect a different result.
  • Change happens in a moment. Robbins argues it doesn't take years to change. It can happen the instant you truly decide, as long as you know how to make the decision stick.
Your assignment for the day

Write down what you want from the next 30 days, as specifically as you can, and commit to doing every daily session. Robbins's point is that the commitment is the first action.

In plain words

Nobody hits the target on the first shot, and that's fine. What separates people who get results is that they keep shooting and keep adjusting. Clarity tells you where to aim; feedback tells you how to correct.

Session 2 · Day 2 · The two forces behind every choice

The Controlling Force That Directs Your Life

In short

Everything you do, you do either to avoid pain or to gain pleasure. Whatever you link pain and pleasure to decides your habits, and you can choose those links on purpose.

The big ideas

  • Pain and pleasure run the show. Every decision, from what you eat to whether you call that client, is your nervous system weighing what it thinks will hurt against what it thinks will feel good.
  • Avoiding pain usually wins. People will do more to avoid losing something than to gain the same thing. That's why fear of rejection often beats the pleasure of a possible yes.
  • Short term beats long term. We procrastinate because the pain of acting is immediate and the pain of not acting is far away. Most bad habits are short-term pleasure with a long-term bill.
  • Change the links, change the behaviour. Once you link enough pain to staying the same and enough pleasure to changing, you'll change. Willpower isn't required; the scales simply tip.
Your assignment for the day

Pick one thing you've been putting off. Write down the pain you link to doing it, the pleasure you get from not doing it, then what it will cost you if you never do it, and what you'll gain if you do.

In plain words

If you keep not doing something you say you want, it isn't laziness. Right now, to your brain, not doing it feels better. You change that by making the cost of staying the same feel real and the reward of changing feel close.

Next: Taking control →
Session 3 · Day 3 · Clarity and leverage

Taking Control: The First Step

In short

To take control of a pattern you first need to know exactly what you want instead and get real leverage on yourself: reasons so strong that changing becomes a must rather than a should.

The big ideas

  • Decide what you want, and what's stopping you. Vague wishes produce vague results. Name the new behaviour precisely, and be honest about what you get out of the old one.
  • Shoulds don't change anything. We all have a list of things we should do. We only change when something becomes a must, when our standards rise and falling short is no longer acceptable.
  • Leverage is pain you can't ignore. Ask what this pattern has already cost you, what it will cost in five years, and who else pays for it. Feel the answer instead of just thinking it.
  • Use other people as leverage too. Telling someone you respect what you're committing to raises the stakes. Backing out now costs you something you care about.
Your assignment for the day

Take one pattern you want to change and write the leverage: what it has cost you, what it will cost in five years, and what changing will give you. Read it aloud until it moves you.

In plain words

You've probably decided to change things many times before. A decision without leverage fades by Thursday. Leverage is the honest, emotional answer to: what happens if I don't?

Next: Conditioning (NAC) →
Session 4 · Day 4 · Neuro-Associative Conditioning, step by step

The Science of Success Conditioning

In short

NAC is Robbins's six-step method for rewiring a habit: decide what you want, get leverage, interrupt the old pattern, install a new choice, condition it until it's automatic, and then test it.

The big ideas

  • Habits are grooves. Every time you repeat a pattern, you link the trigger to the response more firmly. The link runs whether or not you want it to.
  • Interrupt, don't argue. You can't reason a strong pattern away mid-flow. Break it with something unexpected and ridiculous: a silly voice, a sudden movement, a different song. A scratched record can't play the same way.
  • Always replace. If you only remove a habit, you leave a gap and the old pattern comes back to fill it. Give the trigger a new, satisfying place to go.
  • Condition, then test. Rehearse the new response with emotion until it fires on its own. Then test it: imagine the old trigger and check what happens.
Your assignment for the day

Run one small habit through all six steps. Interrupt the old pattern the next time it starts, and rehearse the new one at least ten times today.

In plain words

Think of a habit as a path through grass. The more you walk it, the clearer it gets. NAC means blocking off the old path, choosing a new one, and walking the new one often enough that your feet go there without thinking.

Days 5 to 11 · sessions 5 to 9

What Drives You

Week two moves inside. How you feel in any moment, the questions you ask yourself, the values you rank and the beliefs you hold together decide what you will even attempt. Robbins treats each one as something you can inspect and change on purpose.

The idea behind this stretch

Your results come from your actions, your actions come from your state, and your state is something you can choose.

Session 5 · Days 5 to 7 · Change your state, change your results

What Everyone Wants and How You Can Get It

In short

What everyone really wants is to change how they feel. Your state comes from two things you control at any moment: how you use your body, and what you focus on.

The big ideas

  • Behind every goal is a feeling. You don't want the money, the partner or the body for their own sake; you want how you think they'll make you feel. Learn to change the feeling directly and you're far more powerful.
  • Physiology is the fastest lever. Posture, breathing, facial expression and movement change your state in seconds. A depressed person and a confident person don't stand, breathe or speak the same way.
  • Focus is the other lever. Whatever you concentrate on, you feel. Focus on what's missing and you feel lack; focus on what you have and can do and you feel strength.
  • State decides behaviour. You have the same skills on a good day and a bad day. What changes is your state, and with it, what you're capable of doing.
Your assignment for these days

Three times a day, catch your state and change it on purpose: stand up, breathe fully, move, and ask yourself what you're grateful for right now. Notice how fast it shifts.

In plain words

If you want to feel confident, act the way confident people move: stand tall, breathe deep, look up, speak clearly. Your feelings follow your body more than most people think.

Worth knowing

Research on 'power posing' found that posture reliably changes how confident people feel, but claims that it changes hormone levels did not hold up when retested. The feeling is the part Robbins cares about.

Next: The power of focus →
Session 6 · Day 8 · Questions direct your focus

The Power of Focus

In short

Whatever you focus on, you find and you feel. The fastest way to change your focus is to change the questions you ask yourself.

The big ideas

  • You can't notice everything. Millions of details reach your senses each second, and you consciously notice a tiny handful. What you look for decides which handful.
  • Questions steer the spotlight. Ask 'why does this always happen to me?' and your brain will find reasons. Ask 'what can I learn from this?' and it will find lessons.
  • Your quality of life is the quality of your questions. People who handle setbacks well tend to ask better questions automatically: what's good about this, what can I do now, how can I use it?
  • Morning and evening questions. Robbins suggests a fixed set of questions each morning (what am I happy about, excited about, grateful for) and each evening (what did I give today, what did I learn).
Your assignment for the day

For one full day, catch every disempowering question you ask yourself and swap it for one that makes you look for solutions. Write down the three best swaps.

In plain words

Your brain is a search engine that always returns results. Type in a bad question and it will happily give you bad answers. Ask a better question, and the same day looks different.

Next: Values →
Session 7 · Day 9 · The compass you didn't know you had

Values and Beliefs

In short

Your values are the emotional states you most want to move toward and most want to avoid, and the order you rank them in drives every decision you make, often without you noticing.

The big ideas

  • Moving-toward values. States you'd do almost anything to feel: love, freedom, security, adventure, success. Everyone has them; the order is what makes people different.
  • Moving-away-from values. States you'd do almost anything to avoid: rejection, failure, humiliation, loneliness. A high-ranked away-from value can quietly veto your goals.
  • Conflicts cause paralysis. If freedom and security are both near the top, every big decision pulls you two ways. Knowing the order explains why you feel stuck.
  • You can choose the order. Most people inherited their hierarchy from parents, teachers and experience. Robbins asks you to look at it and decide which order would serve the life you want.
Your assignment for the day

List your top moving-toward and moving-away-from values, rank each list, and write one sentence on where your current order helps you and where it hurts you.

In plain words

When you can't make a decision, it's usually because two things you care about point in different directions. Ranking them makes the trade-off visible, and once you can see it you can decide.

Next: Limiting beliefs →
Session 8 · Day 10 · Beliefs are tables with legs

How to Take Complete Control of Your Life

In short

A belief is just an idea with enough references propping it up. Find the limiting beliefs that hold you back, knock out their legs with questions, and build new ones that support you.

The big ideas

  • An idea plus references. Robbins pictures a belief as a tabletop held up by legs. The legs are experiences, things people told you, and conclusions you drew. Enough legs, and the idea feels like a fact.
  • Limiting beliefs feel like truth. 'I'm not a numbers person' or 'people like me don't do that' never announce themselves as beliefs. They show up as limits you take for granted.
  • Questions wobble the legs. Is that reference really evidence? Was it one time or always? What did it cost you to believe it? Doubt is what lets you rebuild.
  • Build the new table. Pick an empowering belief, then gather references: past moments that support it, other people's examples, even vivid imagined ones.
Your assignment for the day

Write down three beliefs that limit you. For each, question its references and write the belief you'd rather hold, with three references that support it.

In plain words

A lot of what you think you know about yourself rests on a few old moments you've never re-examined. Look at them closely and many turn out to be thin. You can choose stronger evidence.

Next: The power of why →
Session 9 · Day 11 · Reasons come first, answers second

The Power of Why

In short

When you have a strong enough why, you will figure out the how. Purpose is what pushes you through the days when the method stops being fun.

The big ideas

  • Why beats how. People with weak reasons look for the perfect method and quit when it gets hard. People with strong reasons try method after method until one works.
  • Dig past the first answer. The first reason you give ('I want more money') is rarely the real one. Keep asking why that matters until you reach something that moves you.
  • Emotional reasons win. A logical reason helps you plan; an emotional reason gets you out of bed. The best whys are about people and feelings.
  • Keep it in front of you. Write your reasons down and read them often. A why you can't remember can't push you.
Your assignment for the day

Take your biggest goal and ask 'why?' at least three times, writing each answer down. Keep the deepest one where you'll see it every morning.

In plain words

If your reason for doing something is weak, the first obstacle will stop you. If the reason matters enough, the obstacle just becomes a puzzle to solve.

Days 12 to 16 · sessions 10 to 12

Designing Your Future

With the inner machinery mapped, the program turns to direction. You write goals in every area of life, then build the daily rituals and triggers that make reaching them feel automatic rather than heroic.

The idea behind this stretch

A goal without a ritual is a wish. Condition the state you need, every day, and the goal looks after itself.

Session 10 · Days 12 to 14 · Three days to design your future

Creating Your Future: The Goal-Getting Workshop

In short

Over three days you brainstorm goals in every area of life, give each a deadline, pick the most important ones, write why they matter, and turn them into a first step you take right away.

The big ideas

  • Brainstorm without limits. Write fast and don't judge: personal growth, career and money, toys and adventures, contribution. Volume first, editing later.
  • Give every goal a timeframe. One, three, five, ten or twenty years. A deadline turns a dream into a target, and seeing your timeframes shows whether you're all short-term or all someday.
  • Pick and explain. Choose the most important goal in each area and write a paragraph on why you're absolutely committed to it. This is where the power of why comes back.
  • Act before the ink dries. Robbins insists on taking a first concrete step toward each top goal straight away, even a phone call, so the goal gets real momentum.
Your assignment for these days

Spend at least ten minutes on each of the four areas, give every goal a timeframe, pick a top goal in each, write why, and take one action on one of them today.

In plain words

Most people spend more time planning a holiday than their life. This workshop is just sitting down and doing it on purpose, in every area, with dates.

Next: Rituals →
Session 11 · Day 15 · Make the good state automatic

Success Conditioning: The Power of Rituals

In short

Success is mostly what you do daily. A ritual is a fixed routine that puts you in a strong state on purpose, so you don't have to wait to feel like it.

The big ideas

  • You get what you condition. Most people's daily rituals happen by accident: checking the phone, complaining over coffee. Those condition a state too, just not a useful one.
  • Start the day on purpose. Robbins's own morning routine mixes breathing, movement, gratitude, visualising goals and spoken affirmations he calls incantations, said with full body and voice.
  • Breathing comes first. He teaches a breathing pattern in the ratio one to four to two: breathe in for one count, hold for four, breathe out for two, ten times, three times a day.
  • Make room for good emotions. Rituals that clear out frustration and worry leave space for gratitude, excitement and determination to take over.
Your assignment for the day

Design a 15-minute morning ritual and do it today and every day for the rest of the program. Include the breathing, three things you're grateful for, and your top goal read aloud.

In plain words

If you wait until you feel motivated, you'll wait a long time. A ritual is the opposite: you do the routine, and the feeling follows.

Worth knowing

Slow, deliberate breathing does reliably calm the body. The specific claims Robbins makes for the 1 : 4 : 2 ratio, such as cleansing the lymph system, aren't backed by research, so treat the ratio as a habit that works for him rather than a medical fact.

Next: Anchoring →
Session 12 · Day 16 · Link a state to a trigger

Anchoring Yourself to Success

In short

An anchor is a trigger, like a gesture, word or song, linked to a state. Create one at the peak of a strong feeling and you can bring that feeling back on demand.

The big ideas

  • You already have anchors. A song that throws you back to a summer, a smell from a grandparent's kitchen, a tone of voice that makes you tense. They formed by accident.
  • Build them on purpose. Get into an intense state, and right at its peak, apply a unique trigger: a fist pump with a word, a squeeze of the hand. Repeat it until the trigger fires the state.
  • Five keys. The state must be intense, the timing must be at the peak, the trigger must be unique, you must repeat it exactly the same way, and you must do it several times.
  • Collapse bad anchors. Robbins also shows how to fire a strong positive anchor alongside a negative one, so the old trigger loses its grip.
Your assignment for the day

Build a 'power move': recall three moments when you felt totally confident, and at the peak of each, make the same unique gesture and say the same word. Fire it before your next challenge.

In plain words

If a song can make you feel sixteen again, a gesture can be trained to make you feel confident. The trick is timing: link it when the feeling is at its strongest, not when it's fading.

Worth knowing

Anchoring comes from neuro-linguistic programming, which has little solid research behind it. Simple cue conditioning is real, though, and athletes use pre-performance routines the same way.

Days 17 to 22 · sessions 13 to 16

Breaking Through

The middle of the month tackles the areas where people most often hold themselves back: their relationship with money, the fears of failing, succeeding and being rejected, and the quiet sabotage that kicks in just before a breakthrough.

The idea behind this stretch

Most of what blocks you is a mixed signal: you want something and also link pain to getting it. Clean up the signal and the brakes come off.

Session 13 · Day 17 · Wealth is a skill set

How to Condition Yourself for Wealth

In short

Most money problems aren't about income. They come from mixed feelings about money and from never learning a few simple rules: earn more by adding value, keep a share of it, invest it, and let time compound it.

The big ideas

  • Mixed associations. If part of you links money to greed, stress or losing friends, you'll sabotage yourself every time you get close to more of it.
  • Add more value. Income follows the value you bring to others. Robbins asks how you could become more valuable to your employer, clients or market.
  • Keep some and invest it. Pay yourself first. A fixed share of every pay cheque, invested automatically, matters more than how much you earn.
  • Time is the multiplier. Starting early beats saving more later, because returns earn their own returns. Delay costs more than most people realise.
Your assignment for the day

Write down every belief you hold about money, good and bad. Then decide on a share of your income to save and invest automatically, starting this month.

In plain words

Getting rich slowly is surprisingly simple on paper: spend less than you earn, invest the rest, and leave it alone for a long time. What gets in the way is usually how you feel about money, not the arithmetic.

Next: Money beliefs →
Session 14 · Day 18 · Find the hidden brakes

Ending Financial Self-Sabotage

In short

Financial self-sabotage happens when you want money but link pain to having it. Spot the beliefs behind it, re-link pleasure to wealth, and the brakes come off.

The big ideas

  • Two signals at once. 'I want to be rich' and 'rich people are crooks' can live in the same head. The result is stop-start progress and money that slips away.
  • Where the beliefs come from. Many money beliefs are inherited: what your parents said at the kitchen table, what your friends joke about. They were never chosen.
  • Re-link with leverage. Use the NAC tools: get leverage on what sabotage has cost you, interrupt the old story when it starts, and rehearse a new one.
  • Money as a tool. Robbins frames wealth as a way to do more good: take care of family, contribute and live by your values. That makes it easier to want without guilt.
Your assignment for the day

Rewrite your three worst money beliefs as empowering ones, and list what holding the old ones has cost you. Read the new versions aloud morning and night.

In plain words

If you feel guilty about wanting money, you'll find ways not to have it. Making peace with money isn't greed; it's removing a hidden brake.

Next: Fear →
Session 15 · Days 19 to 21 · Three days on fear

Overcoming Fears of Failure, Success and Rejection

In short

Fear of failure, of success and of rejection all work the same way: you replay a vivid inner movie of things going wrong. Change how that movie looks and sounds and the fear loses its power.

The big ideas

  • Fear is a picture. When you're afraid, you're imagining something: the room going silent, the 'no', the look on someone's face. The fear is in the picture, not the event.
  • Change the qualities of the movie. Make the image small, far away, black and white and dim. Give it ridiculous music. The content stays the same but the feeling drains.
  • Scramble it. Robbins's erasure technique has you run the memory backwards and forwards quickly with silly sounds, until it feels absurd rather than frightening.
  • Failure is feedback. There is no failure, only results. Rejection is just information about one approach with one person on one day.
Your assignment for these days

Pick the fear that stops you most. Run its movie, then change it: shrink it, drain the colour, add circus music, and play it backwards five times. Then take one action you've been avoiding.

In plain words

Most fear is a film your mind plays on a loop. You can't stop it by force, but you can mess with the projector until the film stops being scary.

Worth knowing

This 'submodality' work is another idea from NLP with limited research behind it. The best-studied treatment for real fears and phobias is gradual exposure: doing the feared thing in small, safe steps, which is what the 'take one action' part of the assignment does.

Next: Confidence →
Session 16 · Day 22 · The success cycle

Eliminating Self-Sabotage and Creating Unstoppable Self-Confidence

In short

Belief, action and results feed each other in a loop. Low certainty means half-hearted action, weak results and even lower belief. Break in with massive action and the loop runs the other way.

The big ideas

  • The cycle. Your belief in your potential decides how much action you take; your action decides your results; your results feed back into your belief.
  • Self-sabotage is the loop running down. Expect to fail, try a little, get a little, and you've 'proved' yourself right. Each lap makes the next one smaller.
  • Confidence is created, not found. You don't wait to feel certain. You act as if you were certain, using your state, and the results build real certainty.
  • Remember your references. List times you were already capable. Each one is evidence that the belief 'I can do this' is reasonable.
Your assignment for the day

Choose one area where you've been holding back, and for the next 48 hours take action as if success were guaranteed. Write down the results, however small.

In plain words

Confidence doesn't come before action; it comes from it. You get confident by doing things and seeing them work, so the way out of a slump is to act bigger than you feel.

Days 23 to 30 · sessions 17 to 20

Mastering Your Life

The last stretch applies everything to the rest of your life: your body's energy, your closest relationships, and how you handle problems. It ends with a personal challenge to realign your values and goals and keep going after the program stops.

The idea behind this stretch

Personal power is the ability to take action. The program ends; the habit of acting on what you know is the part you keep.

Session 17 · Day 23 · The fuel for everything else

How to Increase Your Energy

In short

Without energy, none of the other tools work. Robbins points to two keys he calls the basics of vibrant health: how you breathe, and how much fresh, water-rich food you eat.

The big ideas

  • Energy is the foundation. Fatigue makes everything look harder and makes you more likely to fall into old patterns. Managing energy is managing your state.
  • Breathe properly. Most people breathe shallowly all day. Deep, deliberate breaths a few times a day lift alertness and calm stress.
  • Eat for energy. Robbins urges plenty of water-rich foods like fruit and vegetables, and less heavy, processed food that leaves you sluggish.
  • Move every day. Regular movement, even walking, builds energy rather than spending it. Many people feel tired because they are under-moved.
Your assignment for the day

For the rest of the program, do the breathing ritual three times a day, add a water-rich food to every meal, and get at least 20 minutes of movement.

In plain words

If you're running on fumes, everything feels harder than it is. Small daily habits around breath, food and movement change how much fuel you start with.

Worth knowing

Robbins's nutrition advice in this session draws on ideas like food combining that mainstream nutrition science doesn't support. The broad message, eat more fresh produce, move and breathe deliberately, matches standard health advice; the details are best checked with a professional.

Next: Relationships →
Session 18 · Day 24 · Why relationships fade, and how to keep them alive

The Power of Successful Relationships

In short

Relationships fail when people stop doing what they did at the beginning, and when each partner has different, unspoken rules for what makes them feel loved. Know each other's values and rules, and keep giving.

The big ideas

  • Do what you did at the beginning. Early on, people pay attention, make effort and surprise each other. Once they stop, the relationship stops feeling the way it did.
  • Rules decide whether you feel loved. One person feels loved when their partner spends time with them; another when they hear it said. If you don't know the other person's rules, your efforts can miss completely.
  • Know their values. Learn what your partner most wants to feel and most wants to avoid. It explains most arguments and most moments of closeness.
  • Give more than you expect. A relationship is a place you go to give, not just to get. Looking for what you can add keeps it growing.
Your assignment for the day

Ask someone close to you: what has to happen for you to feel loved, and for you to feel respected? Share your own answers. Then do one thing on their list today.

In plain words

You might be showing love in a language the other person doesn't hear. Ask them what counts, tell them what counts for you, and both of you get far more chances to feel it.

Next: Problem solving →
Session 19 · Day 25 · Questions that turn problems into progress

How to Solve Problems Quickly and Effectively

In short

Problems are a sign of life, not of failure. Get into a resourceful state first, then use a fixed set of questions to move from complaining to solving.

The big ideas

  • State before strategy. In a frustrated state, everything looks unsolvable. Change your state first, then work on the problem.
  • Ask the problem-solving questions. What's great about this problem? What isn't perfect yet? What am I willing to do to make it the way I want? What am I willing to stop doing? How can I enjoy the process?
  • Find the hidden upside. The first question sounds silly, but it shifts you out of threat mode. Almost every problem also holds a lesson or an opportunity.
  • Problems mean you're alive. Robbins jokes that the only people without problems are in the cemetery. The goal isn't no problems, but better problems.
Your assignment for the day

Take the biggest problem you face right now and answer all five questions in writing. Then take the first action that comes out of your answers.

In plain words

When something goes wrong, the questions you ask decide whether you spiral or move. These five are a ready-made set of better questions for the moment you need them most.

Next: Your challenge →
Session 20 · Days 26 to 30 · The last five days, and every day after

Tony's Personal Challenge to You

In short

The program closes by asking you to realign your values with your goals, review everything you've built, and commit to a challenge that keeps you acting long after day 30.

The big ideas

  • Realign values and goals. If your top values and your goals point in different directions, one of them will lose. Adjust the order or the goals until they pull the same way.
  • Review your tools. Formula, pain and pleasure, NAC, state, focus, values, beliefs, rituals, anchors: decide which three you'll use most and how.
  • Make it a lifestyle. Robbins's challenge is to keep doing the rituals and assignments. Thirty days builds the habit; the habit is what changes your life.
  • Share it. Teach what you learned to someone else. Teaching forces you to understand it, and holds you to living it.
Your assignment for these days

Re-read your goals and values, adjust them so they agree, and write a commitment for the next 30 days. Tick off each day you keep it.

In plain words

The real test isn't finishing a program; it's what you still do three months later. Pick a few habits, track them, and keep going.

About the editions

There have been several versions of the program since 1989, and they divide the material differently. This guide follows the 30-day layout Robbins's own site uses today: 20 sessions, some covering a single day and some, like the goal workshop and the sessions on state and fear, spread over several days. Session names are the program's; everything else here is our own summary.

An independent study guide to Personal Power by Tony Robbins (audio program, 1989; Personal Power II, 1993). The explanations, assignments as summarised, example lines and labs are written in our own words; the session titles are the program's. Labs that show numbers are illustrative models, not research data or financial or medical advice.
