The Art of War,
Explained

Thirteen short chapters, written in China about 2,500 years ago, that are still read by generals, coaches, founders and negotiators. Each chapter here gets its own illustration, the idea in plain language, its key principles, a story from history where it was followed or ignored, and its limits. After each part there's a workshop with a hands-on exercise and a quick quiz. Mark a chapter understood and your progress is remembered on this device.

Read it as strategyHow to size up a contest, choose your ground, use surprise and momentum, and win at the lowest cost.
Read it as restraintSun Tzu's real hero is the general who wins without a fight, never acts in anger, and knows when not to attack.
孫The test at the Wu court

The old story goes that King Helü of Wu, having read the thirteen chapters, asked Sun Tzu to prove them by drilling 180 palace women. Sun Tzu split them into two companies under the king's two favourite concubines and explained the commands. When the women laughed, he said unclear orders were the general's fault, and explained again. When they laughed a second time, he said clear orders not obeyed were the officers' fault, and had both captains executed despite the king's protests. After that the companies drilled in perfect silence, and the king made him a general.

An independent study guide to The Art of War, attributed to Sun Tzu. The explanations and illustrations are original, written and drawn in our own words; chapter titles follow Lionel Giles's 1910 English translation, the historical stories are retold briefly, and the exercises are simplified models of the book's ideas, not measurements. Sun Tzu wrote about real war; the modern "use it today" notes are about competition and conflict in everyday life.
